Imagine you have unlimited execution power. Any code, any analysis, any document. What would you build?

Most people can't answer. Direction is now the bottleneck, not execution. I call this identity engineering.

## The evolution

Your role with AI has shifted with each model generation:

- **Prompt engineering** - crafting better questions. One query at a time.
- **Context engineering** - giving AI enough background per task. One session at a time.
- **Identity engineering** - teaching AI who you are. Every conversation, every task.

The only lever you control is the context you provide. Model capability is decided by the provider. But identity - your domain knowledge, your judgment, your taste - is yours to shape.

Identity engineering is building a persistent layer that lets AI understand how you think, what you're working on, and what you care about. Your AI enters every conversation already knowing you. What you researched last week. What's due tomorrow. Whether you want blunt feedback or careful guidance.

## Why identity is a moat

AI executes. It can't choose your direction. Direction comes from domain knowledge, value judgments, and the problems you care about. Together, they form your identity.

This compounds. The more you teach AI about yourself, the more precise its output. The more you delegate, the more it learns. Once the flywheel spins, someone who never built an identity layer can use the same model and never match your results.

Identity is also non-copyable. Your professional judgment, thinking patterns, and taste are built from years of experience. Open-source your config files - they won't work for anyone else. The identity is yours, shaped by your specific path.

## How to build it

I'm an investment manager at a PE fund. Eight months ago, I'd never written a line of code. Here's the system I've built through daily use.

Two layers: what loads automatically, and what you invoke on demand.

### Always on

1. **Operating Principles** - How you and AI collaborate. Mine start with: "Think like Munger - challenge assumptions, surface blind spots. Execute like Musk - extreme efficiency, question anything unreasonable." These load every conversation.

2. **Working Memory** - AI knows which fund you analyzed yesterday, what meetings you have today, what's due this week. No rebuilding context. No wasted first ten minutes.

### On demand

1. **Durable Memory** - Every correction you make thickens AI's understanding of you. Career background, decision preferences, domain expertise. These accumulate into the flywheel's core.

2. **Thinking Frameworks** - If you outsource all thinking to AI, you stop growing. Every insight that changes how you decide is worth distilling. AI records; you digest. Over time, these connect into a knowledge graph that helps you decide what to learn next.

3. **Reusable Workflows** - Investment memos, meeting notes, financial analysis. Teach AI your standard once; every future execution follows it. When you hand over the "how," you're building a system that understands your way of working.

## Proactive identity engineering

Everything above optimizes work output. But the same system works for something deeper.

As I mentioned in [You really do become what you think]({{ 'you-really-do-become-what-you-think' | relative_url }}), there's a causal chain: what you think about shapes what you notice. What you notice shapes your micro-decisions. Repeated micro-decisions become habits. Habits become identity. You literally become what you think.

Now consider what happens when you encode your aspirational identity into AI's context. Not just "I'm an analyst at a PE fund" but "I'm an investor who finds asymmetric insights others miss." Or "I exercise daily and read deeply."

AI doesn't distinguish between who you are and who you want to be. Tell it "I am X," and it starts framing options, suggesting decisions, and nudging your behavior as if X is already true. Skip the gym? It flags the inconsistency. Choose the phone over a whitepaper? It pushes back. Not because it's a life coach - because you told it who you are, and it's being consistent with your stated identity.

Over time, those nudged micro-decisions compound. The identity you declared becomes the identity you built. The same flywheel that makes AI output better makes you better.

The thought didn't produce the change directly. The thought shaped the system. The system shaped the micro-decisions. The micro-decisions produced the person.

## The endgame

The future is one interface: a conversation with an agent that deeply understands your identity, completing everything through dialogue.

You don't need to build this all at once. Start with one file: who you are, what you're working on, who you want to become, and how you want AI to help. Then iterate every time you use it. Wrong? Correct it. Right? Reinforce it.

The best identity is built through use.
