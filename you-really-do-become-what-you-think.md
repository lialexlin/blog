title: You Really Do Become What You Think

A causal chain with concrete mechanisms at every step.

## 1. Thoughts shape attention filters

Your brain processes ~11 million bits of sensory input per second but you're consciously aware of ~50. What you think about determines which 50 get through.

If you think "I'm bad at networking," you'll notice every awkward pause in a conversation and miss the moments where someone genuinely engaged with what you said. Someone who thinks "I'm good at connecting with people" literally perceives the same conversation differently - they notice the engaged moments, not the pauses.

Your beliefs act as a search query on reality. You find what you're looking for.

## 2. Attention shapes decisions (hundreds per day)

You make ~35,000 conscious decisions daily. Most are micro-decisions you don't notice: who to email back first, whether to speak up in a meeting, whether to go to the gym or skip it, what to read before bed.

Someone who thinks "I'm an investor who understands tech deeply" will, when they have 30 free minutes, read a technical whitepaper. Someone who thinks "I'm just an analyst doing a job" will scroll their phone. Neither decision feels momentous. But compounded over a year, one person has read 200+ papers and the other has read zero.

The thought didn't produce the knowledge. The thought produced the micro-decision, which produced the behavior, which produced the knowledge.

## 3. Repeated decisions become identity

Actions repeated enough times stop being choices and become "who you are."

```
Thought -> Attention filter -> Micro-decisions -> Repeated behavior -> Habit -> Identity
```

Once something becomes identity, it's self-reinforcing. You no longer decide to do it; you'd have to decide NOT to do it. The activation energy flips.

Someone who thinks "I'm a person who exercises" doesn't use willpower to go to the gym. They use willpower to skip it. The default changed. But it started as a thought that shaped enough micro-decisions to cross the identity threshold.

## 4. Identity shapes what's available to you

Your identity determines what opportunities you even see, what rooms you walk into, what people take you seriously for.

Two analysts at the same fund. One thinks "I find asymmetric insights others miss." The other thinks "I need to not make mistakes." Over 3 years, the first one has built a reputation for original thinking (because they kept surfacing ideas, some wrong, some brilliant). The second has a reputation for being reliable but replaceable. Same starting point. Different internal narratives. Divergent career trajectories.

## The reverse is also true

This also works in reverse. If you habitually think "I'm not the kind of person who..." - that thought filters your attention, shapes your micro-decisions, solidifies into identity, and closes doors. You literally become the person who can't, because you decided you weren't before you tried.

## What to do with this

The practical version of this idea has three parts:

1\. **Audit your recurring thoughts** - not the ones you choose, but the ones that run on autopilot. Those are the ones steering your micro-decisions.
2\. **The unit of change is the micro-decision, not the grand resolution.** You don't become a deep thinker by declaring it. You become one by choosing the whitepaper over the phone 200 times.
3\. **Identity is a lagging indicator of repeated behavior, which is a lagging indicator of thought patterns.** If you want to change what you become, change what you habitually think about. Everything downstream follows.

The reason this idea feels abstract is because each individual step is tiny and invisible. No single thought, no single micro-decision, no single day matters. But the direction of the compound curve is set by the thought pattern at the top.
